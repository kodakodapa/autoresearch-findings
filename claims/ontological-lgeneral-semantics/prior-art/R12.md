The claim (K18) is the "separating sentence" construction in `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Example "a separating sentence" (`ex:separating`), which uses Definition `def:lgeneral` and Proposition `prop:lgeneral` in the subsection "What the criterion establishes, and what it leaves open". It works as follows:
- The worlds are all infinite 0-1 sequences, every world sees every other, there is one individual, and Pos is empty.
- The carriers are the hereditarily finitely supported objects under the group of coordinate flips. The paper credits this Fraenkel–Mostowski construction to Jech, Ch. 15, and Pitts.
- Fact (a) proves the model is L-general by induction on terms.
- Fact (b) shows that the members of P_σ are exactly the finitary sets.
- Fact (c) shows there are no atoms, so there are no singletons.
- Therefore "A ∃p.(Atom p ∧ p)" fails at every world.

I found no outside source that gives this exact model, i.e. a Fraenkel–Mostowski / nominal Henkin-style model of a higher-order modal language with the global modality, built to separate a nominal-using proof from truth in general models. The underlying ideas, however, are well known, and each piece of the claim has close prior art:

1. Fine (1970) and Holliday (NDJFL 2019) treat atomicity in propositionally quantified S5. Without the atomicity axiom, models exist whose algebra of propositions is atomless. Holliday's Theorem 2 shows that S5Π plus the negation of the atomicity axiom is complete for complete atomless simple S5 algebras. The finitary sets of 2^ℕ are the clopen sets of Cantor space, which form the classical countable atomless Boolean algebra. So on the propositional side, a model with a universal relation where "every world lies in an atom" fails is standard. Its higher-type closure is not covered by these papers.
2. Ding & Holliday (arXiv 2406.16034) observe that Kripke frames validate the atomicity principle At^n, while general (quantifiable) frames do not settle it. Ding's thesis studies Π-logics over S5 with and without atomicity.
3. Permutation models with finite supports are known as Henkin models of second-order logic: Asser (1981) and Gaßner (arXiv 2409.14211, 2409.10276, 2410.23276). Gabbay & Mulligan's "Nominal Henkin Semantics" (arXiv 1111.0089) builds Henkin models of simply typed λ-calculus from finitely supported objects. These cover the technique behind fact (a), but in a non-modal setting.
4. The paper's remark that no finite model can separate matches the standard fact that finite Boolean algebras are atomic.

So the parts are known: atomless propositional domains falsify atomicity, and FM / finite-support constructions give Henkin-closed models. I did not find the combination for this language and purpose. This is "not found in this search", not proof of novelty.

## Related work
- K. Fine, "Propositional quantifiers in modal logic", Theoria 1970, https://philpapers.org/rec/FINPQI: atomicity principle for S5 with propositional quantifiers; non-atomic propositional domains.
- W. H. Holliday, "A Note on Algebraic Semantics for S5 with Propositional Quantifiers", NDJFL 60(2) 2019, https://escholarship.org/uc/item/303338xr: S5Π plus the negation of atomicity is complete for complete atomless algebras. This is the propositional analogue of the claim's model without atoms.
- Y. Ding & W. H. Holliday, "Some General Completeness Results for Propositionally Quantified Modal Logics", https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16034: atomicity principle At^n holds on Kripke frames but not on general frames.
- Y. Ding, "Propositional Quantification and Comparison in Modal Logic" (thesis), https://escholarship.org/uc/item/78w732g8: Π-logics over S5 with and without atomicity.
- C. Gaßner, "Permutation Models of Second Order", https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14211 (and arXiv 2409.10276, 2410.23276): finite-support FM-style Henkin models of second-order logic, following Asser. Same technique, not modal.
- M. J. Gabbay & D. Mulligan, "Nominal Henkin Semantics", https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.0089: Henkin models of typed λ-calculus in nominal (finitely supported) sets.
- P. Fritz & A. Bacon, "The Algebra of Logical Atomism", RSL, https://andrew-bacon.github.io/papers/The%20Algebra%20of%20Logical%20Atomism.pdf: atomic and atomless propositional algebras extended to higher-order models.
- B. ten Cate 2006 and Kaminski & Tiomkin 1996 (NDJFL 37(1)): expressive power of propositional quantification, which defines atoms and nominals over standard models (the source cites ten Cate).
