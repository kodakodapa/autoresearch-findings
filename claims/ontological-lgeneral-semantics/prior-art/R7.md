The claim is the set of "two remarks" plus fact (c) in `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Example "a separating sentence" (`\label{ex:separating}`), within Section "What the criterion establishes, and what it leaves open" (`sec:criterion-open`). It uses Definition `def:lgeneral` (L-general model) and the definitions of D, Atom and @ that come just before it. I read the claim in the source in four parts:
(i) The sentence A∃p.(Atom p ∧ p) holds in every L-general model with finitely many worlds, because the finite intersection of the admissible propositions containing w is an atom. It also holds in every model whose propositional domain contains all singletons.
(ii) On a universal frame, variant 2 proves modal collapse without nominals (`thm:consequences`(b)). So X ⊆ □X, and the only admissible propositions left are ∅ and W.
(iii) The flip model, built by the Fraenkel–Mostowski / finite-support method over infinite 0-1 sequences, is L-general and has no atoms.
(iv) So that model refutes the sentence, and it is not a model of variant 2.

The mathematical core is well known in the literature on propositionally quantified modal logic (PQML). The sentence is in substance the "world proposition" atomicity axiom of Fine (1970) and Kaplan (1970): some true proposition entails every truth. Here it is stated with the global modality instead of S5's box. In the algebraic and general-frame semantics of PQML it is standard that:
- this axiom holds exactly when the algebra of admissible propositions is atomic;
- every finite Boolean algebra is atomic, which covers the finite-world remark;
- full or Kripke models, and any domain containing the singletons, validate it;
- atomless algebras refute it.

Holliday (NDJFL 2019, Theorem 2, which credits Fine and Kaplan for one part) shows that S5Π plus axiom (W) is complete for complete atomic algebras, and S5Π plus ¬(W) is complete for complete atomless algebras. Ding & Li (arXiv 2406.16034) and Fritz (*Propositional Quantifiers*, 2024; JSL 2024) discuss the same atomicity principle and its failure on general frames. Muskens' "Higher Order Modal Logic" (following Andrews) notes that singletons can be missing from Henkin-style models and that nominals enforce them. The SEP entry on higher-order logic describes Fraenkel–Mostowski permutation models as a standard way to build Henkin models.

What I did not find stated anywhere:
- this particular finitely-supported flip model, used as an L-general model for a HOL embedding of higher-order modal logic;
- its use to separate what the embedding proves with nominals from what holds in L-general models;
- the remark tying variant 2 of Gödel's argument (collapse on a universal frame forces the algebra {∅, W}) to the failure of the atom sentence.

The variant-2 remark follows directly from modal collapse, which the Notes and the cited papers already establish. The verdict is therefore partial: the facts about the atom axiom are standard prior art, and the specific combination and its application are not documented in what I searched.

## Related work
- K. Fine, "Propositional quantifiers in modal logic", Theoria 36 (1970), and D. Kaplan (1970): introduce the world-proposition axiom (W) for S5 with propositional quantifiers. It is the S5 counterpart of A∃p.(Atom p ∧ p).
- W. H. Holliday, "A Note on Algebraic Semantics for S5 with Propositional Quantifiers", NDJFL 60(2) 2019, https://projecteuclid.org/journals/notre-dame-journal-of-formal-logic/volume-60/issue-2/A-Note-on-Algebraic-Semantics-for-S5-with-Propositional-Quantifiers/10.1215/00294527-2019-0001.full: (W) characterises complete atomic algebras, and ¬(W) characterises complete atomless ones. This covers the "atom sentence fails exactly in atomless models" core in the S5 setting.
- Ding & Li (as given in search results), "Some General Completeness Results for Propositionally Quantified Modal Logics", https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16034v1: discusses the atomicity principle and the atomless principle on general frames versus Kripke frames. It notes that Kripke frames validate atomicity.
- P. Fritz, *Propositional Quantifiers* (Cambridge Elements 2024), https://philpapers.org/rec/FRIPQC, and "Axiomatizability of PQML on relational frames", JSL 2024: survey world propositions and atomicity, including Fine's observation that propositional quantifiers can express "true at exactly one accessible world".
- R. Muskens, "Higher Order Modal Logic", https://philarchive.org/archive/MUSHOM: notes that in Henkin-style modal models singletons {w} may be absent, and that nominals enforce their definability. This is the gap the flip model exploits.
- SEP, "Second-order and Higher-order Logic", https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-higher-order/: describes the Fraenkel–Mostowski method as a standard construction of Henkin models. It is the method the flip model uses, though not applied there to modal worlds.
- B. ten Cate (2006), cited in the source: expressing nominal-free hybrid sentences with propositional quantifiers. This is the related expressivity background named in `sec:criterion-open`.
