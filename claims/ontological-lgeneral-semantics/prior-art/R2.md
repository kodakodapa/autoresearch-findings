The claim (K2) is that two derivations hold in every 𝓛-closed structure that admits all entities, because their deep derivations use only 𝓛-term ∀E/∃I instances. The first is K1 ("TwoClosed": Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax3, Ax4, Ax1GenTwo and ∃x.⊤ give Ax1Gen, MC and ∃^E G). The second is K14 ("GexL": Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax4, P E_3 and P G give ∃^E G and MC). I found the method and part of the content in print, but not the two statements as given.

**The method is published.** Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals" (sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex), defines 𝓛-general models in Definition `def:lgeneral`. These are Kripke models whose domains are closed under the terms of 𝓛. Proposition `prop:lgeneral`, in Sect. `sec:criterion-open`, proves soundness over these models for any HOL proof in which every term used as an element of an 𝓛-domain is an 𝓛-term. That covers quantifier instances, arguments of signature constants, motives and so on. The section then says every result the article calls hybrid-free meets this condition and holds in every 𝓛-general model of its variant. So the general principle "only 𝓛-term instances ⇒ truth in every 𝓛-closed model" is already there. The condition in the paper is a whole-term one, which is stronger than a check on ∀E/∃I witnesses alone, and the paper keeps the two apart. Example `ex:separating` and Sect. `sec:open` leave open whether the witness criterion alone is enough. If K2's "only ∀E/∃I instances" is meant as the witness criterion and not the whole-term one, the claim depends on exactly that open gap.

**Part of K1's content is published.** In variant 2, the same paper derives the following from Ax1GenTwo, Ax2a, Ax3 and Ax4 (no Ax2b, no frame condition), and certifies each as hybrid-free:
- L_Two, in Lemma `lem:ltwo`;
- G_ex_Two_at, in Theorem `thm:gextwo`;
- "every world has an actual individual" (ae_Two), then G_ex_Two_unconditional and MC_Two_unconditional, in Corollary `cor:gextwo` and the text after it.

Theorem `thm:plain` gives the same results for variant 3′. By Prop. `prop:lgeneral` these hold in every 𝓛-general model. However, the step Ax1GenTwo ⇒ Ax1Gen (Ax1Gen_of_Two / Two_equivalent) is proved there only through nominals (OneWorld, R_Id). Sect. `sec:plain` and Sect. `sec:open` both say explicitly that a hybrid-free proof of this equivalence is open. K1's "give Ax1Gen" part, using Ax2b and ∃x.⊤, is therefore not in the sources; if it holds, it answers one of that paper's open questions.

**K14 is not found as stated.** Theorem `thm:gex` (G_ex) gets ∃^E G from Ax1Gen, Ax2a and Ax4, using L (P G) through Lemma `lem:l2`. Theorem `thm:consequences` gets MC from there. Corollary `cor:gextwo` (G_ex_Two_of_E) uses positivity of E with E ≠ λx.⊤. I found no derivation of ∃^E G and MC from Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax4, P E_3 and P G without any conjunction axiom, and the names E_3, GexL and TwoClosed appear nowhere in the sources or in the web results.

The Lean port (sources/2609.26806/v3/main.tex, around l. 555–565) makes the weaker remark that its proofs instantiate only object-language terms, so its bounds hold in every model whose properties are closed under the object-language operations.
