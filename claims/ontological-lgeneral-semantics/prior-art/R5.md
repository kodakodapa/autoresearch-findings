**K8.** The closest prior work is the programme's own source, `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`. Definition `def:lgeneral` (Section "What the criterion establishes, and what it leaves open", `sec:criterion-open`) defines $\mathcal L$-general models. Example `ex:separating` uses them for an atomless model in which $\mathsf A\exists p.(\mathsf{Atom}\,p\wedge p)$ is false at every world. That model is built with the Fraenkel–Mostowski / Pitts construction over $W=2^{\mathbb N}$, with a total accessibility relation, one individual and empty positivity. The same example then says outright that this model is **not** a model of variant 2, whatever positivity is chosen. The reason given is that MC holds hybrid-free in variant 2 (`thm:consequences`(b)), and under the total relation MC fails for every finitary set other than ∅ and W. Section `sec:open` ("What stays open") lists as open "whether a separating model exists for any of them", meaning the article's axiom sets. So the source covers the method (FM-style atomless general models, the separating sentence, the definition of $\mathcal L$-general model) but not K8's result. K8 claims a separating model of variant 2 itself, made by switching to the identity frame, where MC is trivial, with $P\varphi w=\varphi()w$. If correct, that answers the open question for variant 2. I found no published model of this kind anywhere else.

**K9.** Proposition `prop:lgeneral` ("soundness over $\mathcal L$-general models") already proves a closely related soundness theorem. It covers HOL proofs in the shallow embedding that meet a whole-term condition, which the `#hybrid_all` detector checks (Appendix `app:detector`). From it the source concludes that every result it calls hybrid-free holds in every $\mathcal L$-general model of its variant. That includes `G_ex_Two_at` (`thm:gextwo`) and Corollary `cor:gextwo`, which give ∃^E G and MC under Ax1GenTwo with no frame condition. K9 is a different route to soundness: deep derivations in a certified calculus, with nonempty individual domains. Its stated payoff goes beyond the source in one place. The source lists as open whether the equivalence of Ax1GenTwo with Ax1Gen in variants 2 and 3′ has a hybrid-free proof (`sec:open`, with `sec:plain`, Theorem `thm:plain`). K9 says Ax1GenTwo ⇒ Ax1Gen holds in all such models via a deep derivation. That settles the semantic counterpart of the open question for variant 2. So K9's soundness theorem is partly anticipated (same model class, same kind of conclusion, but for a different proof system), and its application to K1 is not.

The companion port `sources/2609.26806/v3` does not treat $\mathcal L$-general models; a grep of its `.tex` files found nothing relevant. Web searches turned up nothing on atomless or general-model countermodels for the ontological argument outside the programme's own papers. "None found" here means not found in this search.

## Related work
- Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", arXiv 2609.36279 v4 (sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex): defines $\mathcal L$-general models (def:lgeneral) and proves soundness over them for HOL proofs meeting the whole-term condition (prop:lgeneral), which is a closely related analogue of K9. Gives the FM separating model (ex:separating), which is the method behind K8, but states that it is not a model of variant 2. Lists the existence of a separating model of any variant, and a hybrid-free Ax1GenTwo ⇔ Ax1Gen, as open (sec:open).
- Benzmüller & Kirchner, "First-Order Modal Logic in HOL: deep and shallow embeddings with automated faithfulness", https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10880: deep/shallow faithfulness for first-order modal logic only. No propositional quantification, so no $\mathcal L$-general models.
- Muskens, "Higher Order Modal Logic", https://philarchive.org/archive/MUSHOM: general frames ⟨W,R,Π⟩ with Π a Boolean-closed subset of P(W), the standard background for general models of propositional quantification.
- Fritz & Bacon, "The Algebra of Logical Atomism", https://andrew-bacon.github.io/papers/The%20Algebra%20of%20Logical%20Atomism.pdf: algebraic treatment of atomicity of propositions in higher-order logic, background for the role of atomless models.
