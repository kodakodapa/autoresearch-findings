**K13 (countable models and compactness over v4 L-general models).** I found no source that states this result for the L-general models of `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Definition `def:lgeneral`. In that class the domains need only be closed under terms of the object language L, not under all of HOL. The source proves soundness over these models (Proposition `prop:lgeneral`), but it states no compactness or Löwenheim–Skolem theorem for them. I grepped main.tex for "Skolem", "compact" and "countab" and found only unrelated uses. The method behind the claim is well known, though. Henkin-style general models make higher-order logic behave like many-sorted first-order logic, and compactness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem then follow (SEP entries on higher-order and many-sorted logic). For intensional and modal type theory there is Gallin (1975). Muskens, "Intensional Models for the Theory of Types", proves generalised compactness and generalised Löwenheim–Skolem (refutation by a countable intensional model) through a model-existence theorem. Fine (1970) axiomatises propositional quantification over arbitrary Boolean algebras of sets of worlds, and compactness for that class follows. K13 is a version of these known results for a different model class: domains closed under L only, varying existence (`existsAt`), and the hypothesis ∃x.⊤. I did not find it stated for that class. It is likely a routine consequence of encoding these models as many-sorted first-order structures with L-comprehension axioms, but I found no source that writes this out.

**K14 (a variant-2 model with no atom at some world; finite models always have one).** The second half, that every model with finitely many worlds satisfies the sentence, is already in the source. Example `ex:separating` remarks that with finitely many worlds the intersection of the propositions containing a world is itself in the domain and is an atom. The first half goes beyond the source. The Fraenkel–Mostowski model of `ex:separating` refutes the sentence "every world lies in an atom" (A∃p.(Atom p ∧ p)), but the source says explicitly that this model is not a model of variant 2, because of modal collapse. Section `sec:open` lists as open whether a separating model exists for any of the article's axiom sets. K14 claims to answer that question for variant 2, and I found no published answer. A closely related result is in propositional modal logic. Ding (AiML 2018) observes that atomless complete simple S5 algebras validate S5Π yet falsify ∃p(p ∧ atom(p)), and that full Kripke frames force atoms. Fine (1970) also discusses atoms under propositional quantification. Neither source covers the higher-order setting, Gödel's axioms or the difference-modality definition of Atom used here.

## Related work
- Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", arXiv 2609.36279v4 (sources/…/v4/main.tex): this is the source of K14's finite-worlds half (remark in `ex:separating`). It gives an atomless separating model that is not a model of variant 2, and `sec:open` leaves the variant-2 case open. It proves soundness over L-general models but no compactness or Löwenheim–Skolem theorem.
- R. Muskens, "Intensional Models for the Theory of Types", https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608571: generalised compactness and countable-model Löwenheim–Skolem for intensional general models of type theory. This is the K13 pattern for a different model class.
- D. Gallin, *Intensional and Higher-Order Modal Logic* (1975), via Muskens, https://philarchive.org/archive/MUSHOM: Henkin-style completeness for higher-order modal logic, from which compactness and Löwenheim–Skolem follow.
- K. Fine, "Propositional Quantifiers in Modal Logic", Theoria 1970, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1755-2567.1970.tb00432.x: propositional quantifiers over arbitrary Boolean algebras of sets of worlds, and an atom formula. Covers the propositional analogue relevant to K13 and K14.
- Y. Ding, "On the Logics with Propositional Quantifiers Extending S5Π", AiML 12 (2018), https://philarchive.org/archive/DINOTL: atomless S5 algebras falsify ∃p(p ∧ atom p), while Kripke (full) frames force it. This is the propositional, axiom-free analogue of K14.
