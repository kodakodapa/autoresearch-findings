None of the sources I found states K3, K4 or K6 in the form requested. These claims concern a calculus `Deriv T Cert` ("K1"), "closed structures" whose admitted functions may or may not respect eqT, and a "supported and rel-self-related" sub-carrier for flip-style symmetries. The underlying ideas are standard, though, and the closest source is the programme's own v4 paper.

**Programme source.** `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex` contains three relevant pieces:
- Definition `def:lgeneral` (𝓛-general model) defines models closed under 𝓛-terms.
- Proposition `prop:lgeneral` (soundness over 𝓛-general models) proves soundness for HOL proofs that meet a whole-term condition. In its proof, equality at an 𝓛 function type is read as agreement on P. Constants and variables are extended "by restriction" so that arguments agreeing on P get the same value. That is exactly the respect-eqT condition that K3/K4 make explicit. The paper does not give a structure in which the condition fails, and it does not give a Leibniz rule at every type or a K1-style calculus.
- Example `ex:separating` (Section `sec:criterion-open`) builds the flip/Fraenkel–Mostowski model on W = 2^ℕ with one individual and the hereditarily finitely supported carriers. It shows there are no atoms, so A∃p.(Atom p ∧ p) fails. That is the last clause of K6. The "rel-self-related" refinement and its agreement with support carriers at σ, τ, τ→σ are not in v4. The paper only states closure under 𝓛 ("rest on (a)" for higher types).

**Classical HOL literature.** The general phenomena behind K3/K4 are known:
- Andrews (1972) built a Henkin general model in which extensionality, stated with Leibniz equality, fails because the sets are too sparse.
- Benzmüller, Brown and Kohlhase (2004) classify models by Boolean and functional extensionality. Their "property q" is the condition under which Leibniz equality coincides with identity, and their framework covers a primitive equality and Leibniz equality side by side.

K3 is a specific instance of the familiar failure: congruence of a primitive or defined equality under application at σ→σ fails when function values need not respect the relation. The "self-related elements of a logical relation" construction in K6 is the standard hereditarily-extensional / PER (Gandy-style) collapse. I found no source that combines it with FM carriers over 2^ℕ or with propositionally quantified modal logic plus the global modality.

So the ideas are known (non-extensional general models, Leibniz versus identity, self-related elements of a logical relation, FM models). The specific statements about K1's calculus and the flip-symmetry carriers were not found in this search.

## Related work
- C. Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", arXiv 2609.36279v4 (sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex). `def:lgeneral` and `prop:lgeneral` give soundness over 𝓛-closed models, with equality read as agreement on P and functions extended by restriction (the respect-eqT idea, assumed rather than separated). `ex:separating` gives the FM flip model on 2^ℕ refuting A∃p.(Atom p ∧ p). There is no non-respecting counterexample, no Leibniz rule at every type, and no rel-self-related carriers.
- P. B. Andrews, "General models and extensionality", JSL 37(2) 1972, https://doi.org/10.2307/2272982. A general model in which Leibniz-stated extensionality fails because the sets are sparse. This is the classical form of the K3 phenomenon.
- C. Benzmüller, C. E. Brown, M. Kohlhase, "Higher-order semantics and extensionality", JSL 69(4) 2004, https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/volume-69/issue-4/Higher-order-semantics-and-extensionality/10.2178/jsl/1102022211.short. Model classes with and without functional extensionality, property q, and Leibniz versus primitive equality. This is the general framework for K3/K4.
- W. Degen & J. Johannsen, "Cumulative Higher-Order Logic as a Foundation for Set Theory", https://www.ifi.lmu.de/institut/personen/jjohannsen/jj_papers/kumlog.pdf. A quotient of Henkin structures under Leibniz equality, related to the self-related/collapse construction in K6.

Fritz, "Propositional Quantifiers" (Cambridge Element), https://philpapers.org/rec/FRIPQC, and "Nonconservative extensions by propositional quantifiers…", https://academic.oup.com/logcom/advance-article/doi/10.1093/logcom/exae043/7724499. Quantifiable general frames (domains closed under definable formulas) for propositionally quantified modal logic. This covers only the propositional level of the 𝓛-closure condition.
