Basis: source

The claim (K1) is a soundness theorem for a deep natural-deduction calculus for the HOL of the embedding. Fix a set T of 𝓛-sentences. Take a derivation from T in which every ∀-elimination or ∃-introduction instance at an 𝓛-type is the translation [[t]] of an 𝓛-term t, and in which Leibniz equality is used only where relativised equality is identity. Then the conclusion holds in every 𝓛-closed structure of T that admits all entities.

The closest prior statement is in the programme's own sources: `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Section "sec:criterion-open" (the semantic question). Definition `def:lgeneral` (𝓛-general model) defines Kripke models with domains P_τ closed under 𝓛-terms with parameters. Proposition `prop:lgeneral` (soundness over 𝓛-general models) proves that a HOL proof of ⟦φ⟧ from T holds in every 𝓛-general model of T. Its proof is informal, in prose. It interprets non-𝓛 types fully, relativises quantifiers and equality at 𝓛-types to P, and extends constants and variables by restriction. It then checks ∀E, ∃I, ∀I, ∃E, equality substitution (with the Leibniz case λx. h x = h s ruled out) and extensionality rule by rule. That is essentially the K1 argument. There are two differences:

- **A stronger hypothesis on proofs.** The proposition is stated for HOL proof terms (Lean/Isabelle), not for a deep ND calculus. Every term used as an element of P must be an 𝓛-term, and that includes arguments of signature constants and of variables, motives, and so on. K1 restricts only the ∀E/∃I instances plus Leibniz use. The paper itself notes that the witness-only criterion does not give the proposition, and leaves that question open ("for the witness criterion as such the question stays open"; also Section "sec:open").
- **No formalisation.** Only the propositional part of the separating example `ex:separating` is formalised (Separation.thy / Separation.lean). Proposition `prop:lgeneral` itself is not mechanised, and the paper has no deep ND calculus for it.

`sources/2609.26806/v3/main.tex`, Section "sec:axioms" (around the discussion of countermodels of the Notes), says without proof that the port's bounds hold "for every model whose properties are closed under the object-language operations". The reason it gives is that instantiations use only object-language terms.

Outside the programme, the general principle is textbook material. A HOL / second-order calculus is sound over Henkin (general) structures closed under definability. Restricting instantiation to a fragment needs closure only under that fragment. Sources: Hetzl's lecture notes (Henkin-L-structure, Def. 3.19), the SEP entry on higher-order logic, Rossberg and Bueno on how Henkin models trade off against comprehension, and van Benthem on predicative comprehension and Henkin models. None of them treats the modal embedding's types, worlds as a non-𝓛 type, or the Leibniz restriction. Benzmüller & Kirchner's MSO paper (arXiv 2609.07345) mechanises deep vs shallow faithfulness against comprehension-closed vs general MSO readings, without primitive equality. It has no restricted-instantiation ND soundness theorem.

I found no published result that states K1 itself: a deep ND for the embedding's HOL, with soundness over 𝓛-closed structures under an instance-only restriction. That means not found in this search, not that the result is new.
