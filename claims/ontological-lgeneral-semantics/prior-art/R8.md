Verdict: partial
Basis: source

The claim K12 is a two-way result, soundness plus completeness, for the hybrid-free ("certified") calculus over the shallowly embedded higher-order modal language L. The semantics is L-general models: Kripke models whose type domains P_tau need only be closed under the terms of L, not under every term of HOL, here with a nonempty domain of individuals.

The primary source gives only one direction. `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Definition `def:lgeneral` (Section "criterion-open", around line 1902), defines L-general models. Proposition `prop:lgeneral` ("soundness over L-general models") proves that a HOL proof meeting the whole-term condition derives a sentence true in every L-general model of T. That is the right-to-left direction of K12. The paper does not prove completeness for this class:
- The introduction's contributions list (around line 162) describes the criterion "with the soundness it has and the completeness it lacks".
- Section `sec:open` leaves the semantic question open.
- Example `ex:separating` builds a Fraenkel–Mostowski model, which is L-general but not Henkin. It separates provability in the embedding from truth in L-general models. This shows that the existing Henkin completeness theorems of Benzmüller–Paulson (cited there as [R45, J26], Thm 4.4/4.9) cannot be reused unchanged: their Henkin models must contain every singleton (nominal), and L-general models need not. The `sources/2609.26806/v3/main.tex` port paper has no completeness result of this kind; a grep for faithful/Henkin/completeness found only an unrelated "faithful re-implementation" remark.

In the wider literature, Henkin-style completeness for higher-order modal and intensional logic over general models is classical:
- Gallin 1975, for IL/TY2.
- Fitting's intensional type theory tableaux (2002).
- Muskens' MTT with intensional models (2007).
- Areces–Blackburn–Huertas–Manzano, hybrid type theory. That proof uses nominals, which is exactly what L excludes.

Benzmüller–Paulson prove the shallow embedding sound and complete relative to Henkin semantics. Benzmüller–Kirchner give faithfulness results for first-order modal logic (2607.10880) and for MSO under general semantics (AFP MSOinHOL). None of these states the theorem in K12. That theorem is completeness of the hybrid-free fragment of the HOL embedding with respect to models closed only under L-terms. Its closest analogues are (a) the classical general-model completeness for an object-level HOML calculus and (b) the soundness half in 2609.36279. So the verdict is partial. "Not found" here means not found in this search, not that the result is known to be new.

## Related work
- Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", arXiv 2609.36279 (v4, `prop:lgeneral`, `ex:separating`): the soundness direction of K12 over L-general models, plus a separating example. It explicitly does not prove completeness.
- Benzmüller & Paulson, "Quantified Multimodal Logics in Simple Type Theory", Logica Universalis 7 (2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2435: soundness and completeness of the HOML shallow embedding relative to Henkin models, not L-general models. 2609.36279 notes the theorem needs a closure clause.
- Gallin, *Intensional and Higher-Order Modal Logic* (North-Holland 1975): Henkin-style completeness of an axiomatised intensional higher-order logic over general models. It is an object-level calculus, not the embedding fragment.
- Muskens, "Higher Order Modal Logic", https://semanticsarchive.net/Archive/WQ0ODMxM/homl.pdf: generalized completeness of MTT over intensional (general) models via model existence, building on Fitting's intensional type theory.
- Areces, Blackburn, Huertas, Manzano, "Completeness in Hybrid Type Theory", https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235801245_Completeness_in_Hybrid_Type_Theory: Henkin completeness for higher-order modal logic with nominals and @, i.e. with exactly the resources K12's calculus excludes.
- Benzmüller & Kirchner, arXiv 2607.10880 and AFP MSOinHOL (2026): mechanised deep↔shallow faithfulness for first-order modal logic and for MSO. The MSO entry identifies range-relative validity with general (Henkin) semantics, an analogue for a non-modal second-order language.
