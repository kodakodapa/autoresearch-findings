K15 (soundness) is in substance already in the programme's own source. `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Section "What the criterion establishes, and what it leaves open" (sec:criterion-open), has Definition `def:lgeneral` (L-general model). It also has Proposition `prop:lgeneral` (soundness over L-general models), which states this: if a HOL proof of [[φ]] from an L-theory T uses only L-terms with L-typed parameters wherever a term is used as an element of an L-type domain, then [[φ]] holds in every L-general model of T. Those positions are quantifier instances, arguments of signature constants and variables, "in a hypothesis, a motive, a side of an equation". The proof covers ∀E/∃I, the Leibniz/substitution rule and extensionality case by case. It also explains why a Leibniz motive with a non-L parameter has to be excluded. K15 restates this result in a more specific form: Leibniz at all types, whole-term motives, L-term sides, nonempty individuals. It is a sharper formulation of Proposition `prop:lgeneral`, not a new kind of result. I found no external publication of it.

K16 (completeness, and the three-way equivalence) is not stated in any source I found. The same paper says explicitly that the completeness results it knows of are relative to Henkin models of HOL. Those domains contain every singleton, so they are not L-general models (main.tex, paragraph after `ex:separating`, discussing [J26] Thm 4.4/4.9). Example `ex:separating` shows that unrestricted HOL derivability and truth in L-general models come apart. Section `sec:open` leaves the semantic question behind the criterion open. The external literature has completeness of object-level intensional/higher-order modal calculi relative to Henkin-style general models: Gallin 1975, Fitting 2002 and Muskens 2007. That plausibly covers a "calculus ⇔ general-model validity" leg, depending on what "K4's calculus" is. It also has faithfulness of the HOML→HOL embedding relative to Henkin semantics (Benzmüller & Paulson 2013). I found no result that the whole-term-restricted HOL derivations are complete for, or equivalent to, an object calculus over models whose propositional domains need not contain singletons. "none" for this part means only that this search did not find it.

## Related work
- Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", arXiv 2609.36279 v4, Proposition prop:lgeneral: soundness of HOL proofs meeting a whole-term L-term condition (motives and equation sides included) over L-general models. This is essentially K15. Example ex:separating separates unrestricted HOL provability from L-general validity. There is no completeness result, and the question is listed as open.
- Benzmüller & Paulson, "Quantified Multimodal Logics in Simple Type Theory", Logica Universalis 2013, https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2435: the embedding is sound and complete with respect to Henkin semantics, not L-general models.
- D. Gallin, Intensional and Higher-Order Modal Logic (1975): an axiom system for intensional logic, complete for Henkin-style general models. It is an object-level analogue of one leg of K16.
- M. Fitting, Types, Tableaus, and Gödel's God (2002), https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-94-010-0411-4: tableau completeness for (generalised) Henkin models of classical and intensional type theory.
- R. Muskens, "Higher Order Modal Logic" (Handbook of Modal Logic, 2007), https://philarchive.org/archive/MUSHOM: general models ⟨W,R,Π⟩ with Π⊆P(W), and generalised completeness. It notes that singletons may fail to exist in such models.
- Benzmüller & Kirchner, arXiv 2607.10880: deep↔shallow faithfulness for first-order modal logic only, where the singleton issue does not arise.
