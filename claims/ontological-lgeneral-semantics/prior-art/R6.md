K10 makes two claims. (1) In every L-general model of Ax2a, Ax2b, P E, plain Ax4 and ∃x.⊤, Ax1GenTwo and Ax1Gen are equivalent (variant 2, and variant 3′). (2) In variant 3′, Ax1GenTwo gives MC and ∃^E G. The closest source is Benzmüller, "Proofs Without Nominals", which is in `sources/` (v4 is the latest). I read the relevant sections of its LaTeX.

**Part (2) is covered.** See `sources/2609.36279/v4/main.tex`, Theorem `thm:plain` ("Ax1GenTwoVariant3Plain", Section `sec:plain`; the axioms are listed in Figure `fig:emended`). It proves, under Ax1GenTwo with no frame condition, that a God-like being actually exists at every world (G_ex_Two_unconditional), and that Th3, Th5 and MC hold (MC_Two_unconditional). Both proofs are hybrid-free. Section `sec:criterion-open`, Proposition `prop:lgeneral` (with Definition `def:lgeneral`), is a soundness theorem over L-general models for proofs that meet the whole-term condition. The paper says every result it calls hybrid-free meets that condition. Together these give MC and ∃^E G in every L-general model of variant 3′ with Ax1GenTwo. One difference: the paper proves that every world has an actual individual (ae_Two), while K10 assumes ∃x.⊤.

**Part (1) is stated in the source, but only through nominals. The object-language version is listed there as open.**
- Section `sec:plain` proves the equivalence (Ax1Gen_of_Two, Two_equivalent) by two nominal arguments, OneWorld and R_Id. It says the same argument works in variant 2 using only Ax2a, Ax3 and Ax4 (session E).
- The paper then says it is open whether the equivalence holds hybrid-free. Section `sec:open` repeats this as an open question for variants 2 and 3′.
- Section `sec:assessment` (around the table `tab:verdicts`) adds that no model finder over the embedding can settle this.

So a nominal-based proof does not give the equivalence in L-general models. Those are models whose propositional domains need not contain the atoms a nominal denotes. Claim (1) over L-general models is exactly the question the paper leaves open. If K10 has a proof, it answers that question and is not covered by the paper.

Two smaller points:
- In variant 3 as the Notes state it (not 3′), a genuine model separates the two readings (Two_strictly_weaker, session F). K10's restriction to variant 3′ is therefore needed.
- K10's hypotheses (Ax2b, ∃x.⊤) differ slightly from those of Two_equivalent (Ax2a, Ax3, Ax4).

Web searches found no other work on the Ax1GenTwo/Ax1Gen equivalence, hybrid-free or in general models. The arXiv HTML pages of 2609.36279 (v1, v2) and 2609.26806 only appeared as search results; I did not open them. The verdict rests on the v4 source.
