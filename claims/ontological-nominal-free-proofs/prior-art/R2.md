The claims (K1 for `Ax1GenTwoVariant2`, K2 for `Ax1GenTwoVariant3Plain`, both in logic K) are that all eight named results have proofs of the same statements that v4's hybrid-witness detector passes in witness mode and in whole-term mode. Those results are Ax1Gen_of_Two, Two_equivalent, P_of_conj, loc_of_conj, ae_Two_nominal, G_ex_Two_unconditional_nominal and only_top_and_empty_domain(_False). The prior art is the v4 source itself (arXiv 2609.36279v4, the latest version, posted 2026-10-14). It covers only part of the claim.

What v4 already has:
- In v4's own audit, every one of the named results inherits hybridness through its proof in both modules (`sources/2609.36279/v4/anc/OpenQuestions/audit/report.txt`, the "inherit hybridness through their proofs" lists for both modules). They are flagged, not passed.
- v4 already gives detector-certified proofs of the same statements for two of them: `ae_Two` (the same statement as `ae_Two_nominal`) and `G_ex_Two_unconditional` (the same as `G_ex_Two_unconditional_nominal`). See `anc/OpenQuestions/lean/OpenQuestions/Ax1GenTwoVariant2.lean`, the section "The world left aside, and what a nominal shows about it", where the module docstring says the detector certifies both. The same pair appears in `Ax1GenTwoVariant3Plain.lean` (ae_Two at l. 460, G_ex_Two_unconditional at l. 478). The paper text covers this in `main.tex`, §"Ax1GenTwo: at least two different conjuncts", after Corollary `cor:gextwo`, which calls the argument hybrid-free. For these two results the claim is therefore already in v4.
- v4 does not state a hybrid-free proof of `only_top_and_empty_domain(_False)`. Its hypotheses do contradict the conclusion of `ae_Two`, so such a proof is a one-line corollary of v4's material. v4 itself only proves it through `OneWorld`, which is flagged.
- For Ax1Gen_of_Two, Two_equivalent, P_of_conj and loc_of_conj, v4 says no hybrid-free proof is known:
  - `main.tex` (after `cor:gextwo`): the steps through R_Id and back to the full Ax1Gen "have no hybrid-free counterpart here".
  - §`sec:plain`: "whether the equivalence holds hybrid-free is open".
  - §`sec:open` ("What stays open"): lists whether the equivalence of Ax1GenTwo with Ax1Gen in variants 2 and 3′ has a hybrid-free proof as an open question.

So the core of K1/K2 is a hybrid-free equivalence of Ax1GenTwo and Ax1Gen in K, together with its lemmas P_of_conj and loc_of_conj. The latest source lists it explicitly as open, and the web search found nothing that settles it. Two of the eight statements are already done in v4, and a third follows directly from v4. The verdict is `partial`. For the remaining five, this means only that nothing was found in this search, not that the result is proved to be new.
