**K4.** Most of the claim is already in the source it extends. `sources/2609.36279/v4/anc/OpenQuestions/lean/OpenQuestions/GoedelVariantHOML3inS4oneFile.lean` has hybrid-free `_K` proofs that use no frame condition. Each `_K` lemma has the same statement as the theorem it stands in for:

- `Th5_K`, `Th3_K`, `MC_K`, `MCdual_K`, `Triv_K`
- `UniqueEss3_K`, `UniqueEss1_K`, `UniqueEss2_K`
- `EssMemI_K`, which gives `EssMem` in one step
- `Ess_iff_Ess2_K`

`AllExist` is already proved from `G_ex` and `Serial`, without `Rrefl`. The docstring of `Serial` says these proofs "hold in K outright". The paper says the same: main.tex, Lemma `lem:serial`, Lemma `lem:reach`, Theorem `thm:uniqueess-k` and Theorem `thm:v3`. The last of these reports that `AllExist` is hybrid-free and that the detector gives it only the informational ACTUAL flag. The paper also notes that `Serial` removes reflexivity from `NoNecExist`/`OneWorld`, but those proofs use a nominal (`thm:nonecexist`).

So the parts of K4 that v4 does not already contain are these:

- `EssSupp` and `EssDetermined`: proofs that are both hybrid-free and free of `Rrefl`, with `EssSupp` stated for an arbitrary world `v`. In v4, both go through `OneWorld`, which is nominal, plus `Rrefl` (lines 370–420 of the Lean file).
- An `EssMemI` route that does not use `AllExist`.
- An `AllExist` proof that does not carry the ACTUAL flag.

These are refinements built from v4's own tools: `Reach_K` and constant-property witnesses. I found no external source that states them.

**K13.** v4 covers the standard-model case and leaves the general question open. `Ax1GenRigidVariant2.lean` proves `L_R`: Ax1GenR with the family Φ := {ψ | P ψ w} gives P G in K, using Ax2a/Ax2b via `P_succ`. Read contrapositively, this says: if ¬P G w, the rigidified P_w cannot be a family that Ax1GenR may be applied to. K13 places this in Henkin models with admitted families. v4 does not do that. It lists, as an open question, whether L follows under the rigid reading with no condition on positivity (main.tex `sec:rigid`, `sec:open`). It also discusses L-general and Henkin models (`def:lgeneral`, `prop:lgeneral`).

The other half of K13 says that a positive property is realised by an actual entity at an R-successor. This is a form of Gödel/Scott T1 ("positive properties are possibly exemplified"), stated in the Notes and in older literature (Koons; Benzmüller's simplified variants). In K it needs a successor world, which v4's `Serial` supplies (`lem:serial`). I found no published statement of K13 for admitted rigid families in Henkin models. This means not found in this search, not that the result is new.
