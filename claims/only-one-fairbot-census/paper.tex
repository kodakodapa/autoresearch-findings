\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:only-one-fairbot-census}

\subsection*{Setting}

Two programs play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, and each can read the other's source code.
Following Barasz et al. \cite{barasz2014robust}, a \emph{modal agent} decides by asking what can be proved
about the opponent, written with the provability box $\Box$ of the logic GL: $p$ means ``the
opponent cooperates with me'' and $q$ ``the opponent cooperates with DefectBot''. FairBot is
$\Box p$: it cooperates exactly when it can prove that the opponent cooperates with it. An agent
is \emph{unexploitable} if no modal opponent ever ends up defecting while the agent cooperates, and
\emph{self-cooperating} if two copies cooperate. We take guarded formulas (every atom under a box)
built from $p$, $q$, $\bot$, $\neg$, $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\Box$, measure size as the number of
nodes, and call two agents behaviourally equal when they make the same moves against every
opponent. Agents using only $p$ are the rank-0 agents of Barasz et al.

A LessWrong post \cite{onlyonefairbot2026} asked whether every rank-0 agent that is unexploitable and
self-cooperating is FairBot. A comment there shows that the answer is yes up to modal depth 1 and
no from depth 2, with examples such as $\Box\Box p$, FairBot over PA+1 and $\Box^k p$.

\subsection*{Result}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rank 0.} Among rank-0 agents of size at most 15 there are exactly 5031 classes that are unexploitable and self-cooperating (4374 of them also cooperate with FairBot and CooperateBot). Only $\Box p$ itself is FairBot; every other class is told apart from FairBot by an explicit opponent.
\item \textbf{With $q$.} Allowing $q$, up to size 14 there are 26449 such classes (16756 up to behaviour); 387 of them behave exactly like FairBot against every opponent, and each of the others is told apart from FairBot by an explicit opponent.
\item \textbf{Why some different formulas are FairBot.} Two agents behave identically against every opponent exactly when they have the same moves against DefectBot and agree on every input the opponent can actually produce: until the agent first cooperates, the opponent's move against it equals its move against DefectBot. So, for example, $\Box(p \vee (q \wedge \Box\bot))$ is behaviourally FairBot.
\item \textbf{A small example that is not FairBot.} $\neg\Box\bot \wedge \Box(p \vee \Box\bot)$ (size 9) is unexploitable and cooperates with itself and with CooperateBot, yet mutually defects with FairBot; it is the agent WaitFairBot$_1$ of Barasz et al. Every smaller rank-0 agent that is unexploitable and self-cooperating cooperates with FairBot.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Why it matters}

The LessWrong question was answered with a few examples. These counts make the answer
quantitative: among small agents, FairBot is one of thousands of unexploitable self-cooperators,
and for each one there is a concrete opponent that tells it apart from FairBot. The criterion for
behavioural equality explains which syntactically different agents are nevertheless FairBot.

\subsection*{Proved vs checked}

The equality criterion and the FairBot-equivalence of $\Box(p \vee (q \wedge \Box\bot))$ are
proved in Lean 4, in the height semantics of closed GL fixed points; the link to provability in
arithmetic is Solovay's theorem \cite{solovay1976provability}, cited, not formalised (see also
\cite{boolos1993logic}). The counts are exhaustive computations up to the stated sizes, with every
separating opponent recorded and re-solved by the checks. The minimality statement for the size-9
agent is an exhaustive search over smaller rank-0 agents.

\subsection*{Novelty statement}

This result extends Barasz et al. (arXiv 1401.5577): modal agents, unexploitability, rank, and WaitFairBot\_K; ``Is there only one FairBot?'' (LessWrong, 2026), with Zac Hatfield-Dodds's comment answering it to modal depth 1 and giving examples. Our prior-art search (2026-10-21) covered:

\begin{itemize}
\item Barasz, LaVictoire, Christiano, Fallenstein, Herreshoff, Yudkowsky, ``Robust Cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma: Program Equilibrium via Provability Logic'' (arXiv 1401.5577): unexploitability (Section 2), rank and Theorem 4.10 (Section 4), WaitFairBot\_K and the obstacle theorem (Section 5).
\item LessWrong, ``Is there only one FairBot?'' (transhumanist\_atom\_understander, 29 Aug 2026) and comments: Zac Hatfield-Dodds shows the uniqueness conjecture holds up to modal depth 1 for rank-0 agents and fails from depth 2 (Box Box p; FairBot over PA+1 = Box(p or Box False); Box\textasciicircum{}k p for k \textgreater{}= 3); PrudentBot separates Box Box p from FairBot; the author notes Box Box p is not provably unexploitable in PA. \subsection*{Claim K18}

For causal agents $A,A'$ in height semantics: they make the same moves in every game against every causal opponent iff their DefectBot traces are equal and they agree on all realizable words ($p=q$ up to the first cooperation of $A$ or its DefectBot trace).

Status: derived · author check only.

\paragraph{Verified.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{checks/K18-c046\_realizable\_lean.py}: Lean 4 core theorems game\_exists, inputs\_realizable, same\_behaviour, separation, behaviour\_iff, X\_beheq\_FB; SHA-pinned source; no sorryAx; 4 mutations rejected --- pass (Lean-proved, no sorryAx)
\item \texttt{checks/K18-c046\_qfull12.py}: behav() realizable-word machines: 2574 classes, FairBot class has 86 machine classes with identical realizable-word machine to []p (consequence: FairBot behaviour against every opponent via same\_behaviour) --- pass
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Evidence.}
\begin{itemize}
\item reasoning: In any game the opponent's inputs are all-false until the agent or its DefectBot trace cooperates, so by causality its play equals its DefectBot play there; hence inputs are realizable, and by induction equal realizable behaviour gives equal games.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

The counts depend on the agent language and the size measure; they say how common
non-FairBot unexploitable self-cooperators are among small formulas, not in any absolute sense.
``Unexploitable'' is about what actually happens in play (truth in the standard model, as in Barasz
et al.), not about what Peano arithmetic can prove about the agent; the LessWrong discussion
points out that the two differ. Open questions: how the counts grow with size, and whether the
non-FairBot classes fall into a small number of families.
