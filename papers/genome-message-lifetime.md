# {% raw %}How long can a message hidden in DNA be read? Model results from a six-hour AI research run{% endraw %}

Lindheim, Björn · 2026-11-13 · version 1

## Abstract

{% raw %}
Suppose a message is written into non-functional DNA of a living species and protected by an error-correcting code. How long can it still be decoded, from the population or from all descendant species, and how much could be hidden this way? A team of three AI agents worked on this question for six hours in an exploratory research harness. In their models, a 1 kB message in one million bases of human DNA has an information ceiling of 470–890 million years at long-run mutation and DNA-loss rates; an implemented code gives an estimated 380–650 million years, but that decoder fails once realistic unknown-position deletions are added. Those timescales are hundreds of times longer than a mammal species usually lasts. A message placed in the ancestor of birds and mammals 320 million years ago could still carry about 0.2–1.2 MB per 1% of a human-sized genome when read from one descendant, and 1.7–5.0 MB when descendants are read jointly; at four billion years only about two bits remain. A message written into one individual is usually lost: in an idealised population of 10,000 only 1 in 20,000 spreads to everyone, which takes about a million years. These are model results, not forecasts, and the main limitation (the loss rate of an inserted stretch of DNA) is not calibrated. We make no claim of priority. The author has not verified the derivations in detail.
